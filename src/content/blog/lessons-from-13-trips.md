After enough trips, the same lessons come up every time. These are the ones that made it into every spreadsheet.

## 1. Paperwork first

- **Visas take longer than you think.** Türkiye needed a VFS appointment with 6 months of bank statements, salary slips, ITR and a leave NOC. Vietnam and Egypt are e-visas but still take 3–7 days. Azerbaijan's e-visa was the easiest.
- **Domestic permits exist too.** Lakshadweep needs a police verification certificate and an entry permit, so start early.
- **Keep everything offline**: PDFs of flights, hotels and insurance saved to Drive *and* downloaded on your phone.

## 2. Money

- Carry some USD. It's the easiest currency to change everywhere from Hanoi to Hurghada.
- Thailand may ask for **proof of funds** (about USD 700 or THB 20,000 per person).
- A zero-markup forex card saves more than you'd expect.
- Keep a shared expense sheet with a *paid by* column from day one. Settling up afterwards is much easier.

## 3. Apps to install before you land

| Where | Apps |
| --- | --- |
| Everywhere | Offline Google Maps, Google Translate (offline language packs) |
| Southeast Asia | Grab, Bolt |
| Türkiye | BiTaksi, Flixbus |
| Azerbaijan | Bolt, Yandex Go, VisitBaku |

## 4. Pace

- **Two nights per base** is the sweet spot with family (Kashmir and Meghalaya both worked well at this pace).
- Long road transfers are fine if the car is private. Cairo to Port Ghalib took 5.5 hours and was cheaper than flying.
- Overnight buses in Türkiye save both money and a hotel night.
- Always keep one slack day for weather: balloons get cancelled and ferries get delayed.

## 5. The packing list that never changes

Passport + copies, cards, some USD/EUR, universal adapter, power bank, sunscreen, swimwear, a bag for wet clothes, mosquito repellent, a small day bag, medicines (band-aids, paracetamol, cetirizine, something for an upset stomach and seasickness), and a pen for immigration forms.
